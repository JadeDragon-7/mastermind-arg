OFFICIAL ULTIMATE DEFINITION

OVERVIEW

Ultimates were created by world leaders in 1972, to encourage their citizens to be the very best they can be. Each country of the world has the ability to sanction Ultimates, and some large countries such as The United States of America, China, and so on have been subdivided. This means there can be duplicate Ultimates across different countries. The testing and assignment of Ultimates is handled by Offices of Civil Distinction. These offices are spread across countries, usually one existing within each state, country, or large area.

The existence and regulations of Ultimates are complicated so they have been subdivided into several different categories in this document: Testing, Retesting, Reassignment, and Generation.

TESTING

The Testing category typically refers to the "Ultimate Test". To be given an official, government sanctioned Ultimate, one must first apply. The application can be for an already existing Ultimate, a proposal of a new Ultimate, or a request to be assigned to an Ultimate. The applicant will go through a long process of interviews and testing, before receiving their final test, referred to as the "Ultimate Test". If they pass this test, they will be officially recognized as their Ultimate.

There are three different kinds of tests.

1. Existing Ultimate Testing

⊳ The testing for an already existing Ultimate.

⊳ This can often include replacing someone who currently owns the Ultimate.

2. Prospective Ultimate Testing

⊳ This involves proposing a brand new Ultimate, giving time for the government to approve the new Ultimate and develop the testing for said Ultimate, and then passing the test.

⊳ The applicant will never replace someone else, as the Ultimate has never existed prior.

3. Unassigned Ultimate Testing

⊳ This involves going in for testing without choosing a designated Ultimate.

⊳ The government will assign an Ultimate based on the testing, if the applicant scores high enough.

⊳ This is often a lengthy process and requires many different days of testing.

⊳ If an unassigned tester receives an Ultimate, they are required to accept the Ultimate.

⊳ The applicant may or may not replace someone else.

TERMS AND CONDITIONS OF TESTING

There are certain terms and conditions that apply to all the Testing categories.

1. Once applying for a certain Ultimate, an applicant cannot switch and reapply to a different Ultimate.

⊳ In regards to Unassigned Ultimate Testing, an applicant cannot switch from Unassigned Ultimate Testing to Existing Ultimate Testing or Prospective Ultimate Testing.

2. An applicant can only take the Ultimate Test a maximum of three times. If they fail all three attempts, they can never receive an Ultimate. There is no time restriction on how quickly or slowly an applicant can retake their Ultimate test.

3. The minimum age you can apply for an Ultimate is 10 years old. Those born before 1965 are also unable to apply.

4. An individual is required to score a minimum of 95.00% to pass their Ultimate Test.

Many of these terms and conditions above are contingent on the other subcategories.

The Reserved category of Ultimates refers to the applicants who have passed their tests, but are aged 10-14. If someone aged from 10-14 passes their Ultimate Test, it is considered "Reserved" for them until they reach the age of 15, where then they will officially receive their Ultimate.

RETESTING

Every five years, an individual with a registered Ultimate must retest to continue their ownership of an Ultimate. Every retest will have three available attempts, similar to testing. These attempts reset at every new retest. Like Testing, if an individual fails all three attempts, they will no longer own their Ultimate and be unable to test again.

REASSIGNMENT

Reassignment refers to when an Ultimate is successfully taken by a new individual from the current owner of the Ultimate.

1. Ultimates are available to be reassigned one year after the current owner activates their Ultimate.

2. In the event that an applicant successfully passes the Ultimate test for a taken Ultimate, the current owner will be notified and given two months to respond.

3. To keep their Ultimate, the current owner has a single test to score higher than the other applicant. If they score lower or fail, they will forfeit their Ultimate. If they score higher, they will keep their Ultimate.

4. If the challenger is out-scored, the test will not count as one of their three attempts. They must either wait another full year, or receive government assistance to find a similar Ultimate.

⊳ This is not considered "switching" your Ultimate, as the government will choose a similar available option.

GENERATIONS OF ULTIMATES

The categories X, Y, Z, and A refer to Ultimates per generation. The cutoffs are as follows:

X — 1965-1980

Y — 1981-1996

Z — 1997-2010

A — 2010-2024

The categories separate and mark the cutoffs of each Ultimate. Therefore, there can be four of the same Ultimates at the one time within the same region. This also spreads out the competition for certain Ultimates into age categories, so someone who is 40-years-old is not competing with someone who is 16-years-old. It is expected that as generations are created, so will more subcategories of Ultimates.

LEGAL ASPECTS OF ULTIMATES

Countries around the world have set new laws in place about Ultimates. It's important that an applicant familiarizes themselves with their country's laws about Ultimates before proceeding with testing. Below is a list of common laws or regulations across countries.

1. Unanimously across any country, it is illegal to claim, lie about, or fake an Ultimate. Doing so can have serious repercussions, or even incarceration depending on the situation or offense..

2. An individual must be a permanent and legal resident of the region they are testing in.

⊳ If the individual moves regions for any reason, they will forfeit their Ultimate and must retest in their new location.

⊳ If an individual moves during any type of testing, attempts will not reset between regions.

⊳ If an individual moves while currently owning an Ultimate, attempts will reset as it will be considered a retest.

⊳ These rules only apply to permanent moves, and exclusions may be made for temporary residences, work visas, and so on.

3. Some specific Ultimates have age requirements, such as Ultimates involved in age-restricted activities such as drinking, driving, and so on.

⊳ Specific Ultimates may be limited to applicants above the ages of 16, 18, 21, and so on depending on the Ultimate.

⊳ Ultimates involving military or law enforcement may require training or education before application is allowed.

⊳ It's important for an applicant to familiarize themselves with the restrictions of their chosen Ultimate before applying.
